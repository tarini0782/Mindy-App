## 1. Design rationale, with evidence separated from recommendation

The brief asked for credible research and for published evidence to be
distinguished from design opinion. That distinction is kept strictly below,
because the opposite — dressing preference up as finding — is exactly what a
research panel probes.

### Standards (normative, not opinion)

**WCAG 2.1 contrast minimums** are a published accessibility standard:
4.5:1 for body text, 3.0:1 for large text (≥18pt, or ≥14pt bold). This is the
one rule applied here without argument, and §2 reports the measured ratio for
every colour pair the app actually uses.

### Verified from this project's own literature review

- Picture-based self-report has precedent for children from age 3, with
  modest reliability (Venham & Gaulin-Kremer, 1979: test–retest 0.70,
  KR-20 0.838), **but** graded self-report is unreliable at the youngest end
  — von Baeyer (2006) reports that up to three-quarters of typical 3-year-olds
  may not understand a graded task. *Design consequence:* check-in options
  stay pictorial and few, and never depend on reading.
- Digital preparation interventions for children's medical procedures show
  measurable effects but a weak evidence base, with most trials at high risk
  of bias (Almuqad et al., 2026). *Design consequence:* the interface must not
  imply therapeutic effect.

Full citations: `docs/RESEARCH_REPORT.md` §14.

### Design recommendations (reasoned, not evidence)

These are design judgements. They are **not** claims about what children
prefer, and none of them has been tested with a child.

1. **Split the palette by job, not by mood.** No claim is made that teal calms
   or yellow cheers. The split below is driven purely by legibility.
2. **Colour never carries meaning alone.** Every state also has an icon and a
   word, so the indicator cards remain readable for a colour-blind caregiver.
3. **Calm screens get less, not duller.** Calm mode uses the same palette with
   fewer simultaneous elements and no gradient — rather than a greyed-out
   version, which reads as "broken" to a child.
4. **Avoid the preschool trap.** Rounded shapes and clear silhouettes, but
   restrained decoration and real typography, so a 10–12 year-old is not
   addressed as a toddler.
5. **No claim of preference.** Nothing here may be written up as "children
   prefer this theme" without an approved study with children.

## 3. The three visual modes

Same tokens throughout. What changes is **how much is on screen**, not the
palette — so moving between modes feels like one app.

| | Explore | Calm | Caregiver / research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Background | `mist` + world gradient | `mist` only | `mist` |
| Surface | display brights | one soft tint | white cards |
| Gradients | yes (`hero`, `sunrise`, `meadow`) | none | none |
| Motion | bounce, sparkle, parallax | slow breathing scale only | none |
| Density | generous, illustrative | one thing at a time | information-dense |
| Text | large, few words | large, fewest words | normal, precise |
| Decoration | yes | minimal | **none** — a decorative chart could distort interpretation |

Calm mode must never add a timer, penalty, hazard or failure state. That is a
behavioural rule, not a visual one, and it is enforced in the calm-pool
activities already.

## 4. Six-character design sheet

They were creatures — a unicorn with a horn and mane, a baby dragon with
spikes and a tail, a fairy. Drawn as flat shapes those read as little
monsters. All six are now **children**, in the same visual language as the
avatar a child builds for themselves, so hosts and player character finally
belong to one world. Names and personalities are unchanged.

| Character | Personality | Silhouette cue | Prop | Signature | Implemented |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kara** | Welcoming, the guide | long hair | star hair clip | warm skin, chestnut hair, dress-up colour applies | yes |
| **Nova** | Brave, encouraging | cape, short hair | cape | deep skin, black hair, strong blue | yes |
| **Lumi** | Calm, kind | bob | flower crown | light skin, golden hair, lavender | yes |
| **Bubbles** | Playful | two buns | bubble wand | mid skin, dark hair, sky blue | yes |
| **Pip** | Curious explorer | curly volume | explorer cap | deep skin, black curls, meadow green | yes |
| **Twinkle** | Quiet, thoughtful | ponytail | paper lantern | light skin, pink hair, pale blue | yes |

Distinct by **silhouette and prop**, not only colour, so they remain
recognisable in a 28px chip and to a colour-blind child. Skin tones, hair
textures and hairstyles vary across the six; no prop, colour or personality
is assigned by gender.

**Character creator:** unchanged and still functional — 6 skin tones, 6 hair
styles, 8 hair colours, 6 outfits, 6 outfit colours, 6 headwear, 4 wings,
4 faces, 5 extras, 4 presets, up to 6 saved characters per child. No gender
categories anywhere. Persistence, reset and profile separation untouched.

All 27 games and their mechanics are unchanged. This is the spec for a later
stage; nothing in `lib/games/` was edited for visual reasons.

| World | Activities | Palette | Intensity | Research semantics to preserve |

| Firefly Meadow | Firefly Jar | navy + sun | calm | No timer, nothing expires, off-target tap = neutral `touch`, never `mistake` |

| Sky Meadow / Star Sky | Cloud Hop, Turbo Car, Sky Flight, Cloud Bounce | sky + lavender | active | Obstacle timing feeds "What happened after a slip" |

**Rule for implementation:** a game's visual change may touch only colours,
backgrounds and decoration. Hitboxes, timing, target frequency, scoring,
event names and event ordering are out of bounds without a regression test
and a written reason.

Re-measure any colour you change — this is the exact method used above:

```python
def lum(h):
    h = h.lstrip('#')
    c = [int(h[i:i+2], 16) / 255 for i in (0, 2, 4)]
    c = [x / 12.92 if x <= 0.03928 else ((x + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4 for x in c]
    return 0.2126 * c[0] + 0.7152 * c[1] + 0.0722 * c[2]

def ratio(a, b):
    la, lb = lum(a), lum(b)
    hi, lo = max(la, lb), min(la, lb)
    return (hi + 0.05) / (lo + 0.05)

print(ratio('#FFFFFF', '#26837C'))  # 4.55 -> passes body text
```

Check on a phone as well as a tablet: the tokens are size-independent, but
the layouts are not, and nothing here has been seen on a device.
