## 1. Wiley, Robinson & Mandryk (2021) — READ (abstract + Crossref record)
* **Correct citation:** Wiley K, Robinson R, Mandryk RL. *The Making and Evaluation of Digital Games Used for the Assessment of Attention: Systematic Review.* JMIR Serious Games 2021;9(3):e26449. DOI 10.2196/26449.
* **Citation correction:** your list says "Robinson and Mandryk". The first author is **Wiley**.
* Objective: how attention-assessment games are made and evaluated. Method: 6 databases + 100 Google Scholar hits; 74 studies (from 49,365 records) up to 1 Mar 2021, remote-deployable on computer/tablet/phone.
* Findings: 33 gamified existing tasks, 37 custom games from cognition theory, 4 commercial games. Validation: 31 vs a traditional task, 14 vs clinical diagnosis, 11 vs established cognition knowledge, 4 machine learning, 13 none. Only 21 evaluated both assessment and game qualities.
* Limits (authors): not all databases, published work only, attention only.
* **Relevance to Mindy / claim supported:** the field builds assessment games more than it evaluates them, and assumes (without evidence) that games improve assessment. Supports Mindy's *plan to evaluate both rule outputs and game experience*. Does **not** show that Mindy's games work.

## 2. Bryant, McGuire & Sisk (2024) — READ (university press summary only, not the paper)
* **Correct citation:** Bryant B, McGuire JF, Sisk M. *Efficacy of Gamified Digital Mental Health Interventions for Pediatric Mental Health Conditions* (title as searched; JAMA Pediatrics, published 23 Sep 2024). **You still need to confirm title/DOI from the journal page.**
* **Citation correction:** your list says "McGuire 2024". McGuire is senior author; the first author is **Bryant**.
* What was read (Johns Hopkins summary): 27 RCTs, 2,911 participants aged 6–17. Hedges g: ADHD 0.28, depression 0.28, anxiety 0.07. Computer delivery and preset time limits associated with better outcomes. Caveats: parent/child reports, inconsistent engagement measures.
* **Relevance:** about *interventions*, not screening/assessment. Use only to say "gamified tools for children have modest measured effects and engagement is under-measured". Do not use it to justify Mindy as treatment.
